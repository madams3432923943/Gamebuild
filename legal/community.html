Community Guidelines

Version 1.0 — last updated August 13, 2026

In short

Trash talk about a draft pick is the point of the game. Slurs, harassment, sexual content, threats and spam are not. These rules apply to anything other players can see: your username, your squad's name, tag and motto, and squad chat. Breaking them costs you the content, and repeatedly breaking them costs you the account.

Where these rules apply

Everywhere you can put words in front of another player: usernames, squad names, squad tags, squad mottos and squad chat messages. They are part of the Terms of Service.

Not allowed

  • Slurs and hate speech — attacks or dehumanising language aimed at race, ethnicity, national origin, religion, disability, sex, gender identity or sexual orientation.
  • Harassment — targeting a specific person with insults, following them between squads to keep at it, or encouraging others to pile on.
  • Threats and violence — threatening anyone with harm, or wishing it on them. Threats against real people are also reported to the authorities where the law requires it.
  • Sexual content — explicit or suggestive material. Draft Nova is open to players as young as 13.
  • Self-harm content — encouraging or instructing anyone toward suicide or self-injury. If you are struggling, please talk to someone: in the US, call or text 988.
  • Doxxing — posting anyone's real name, address, phone number, workplace, school or other private information, including your own. Squad chat is not private.
  • Impersonation — pretending to be another player, a real athlete, a league, a moderator or us.
  • Spam and advertising — repeated messages, flooding, chain messages, referral links, or promoting a product, stream or service.
  • Cheating talk with teeth — selling accounts, offering boosting, sharing exploits, or organising match fixing.
  • Gambling — arranging wagers on Draft Nova results, or advertising a betting service. See the Terms.
  • Illegal content — anything unlawful, and above all anything that sexualises a minor, which is reported to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children and ends the account permanently.

Allowed, and welcome

Talking trash about someone's roster, arguing about eras, calling a pick indefensible, and celebrating loudly. The line is between the team someone drafted and the person who drafted it.

How this is enforced

Two layers, and the second is the one that counts:

  • In the app, a filter checks usernames, squad names and chat before they are sent, so you get told immediately rather than after the fact.
  • On the server, the same rules run inside the database, and they are what actually decide. A modified client cannot get around them, because the check happens where the data is written, not where it is typed.

Filters are blunt instruments. They will occasionally block something innocent — a real surname, a team nickname, an unlucky substring. If that happens to you, email maxwell@gurrbrothers.com and we will fix the list. They will also miss things, which is what reporting is for.

Reporting

Every squad chat message has a report control. A report tells us the message, who sent it, and why you flagged it. We review reports and may remove the content, warn the player, restrict chat, or terminate the account. We do not tell the reported player who reported them.

For anything urgent — a credible threat, content involving a minor — email maxwell@gurrbrothers.com directly rather than only using the in-game report.

Consequences

Depending on what happened and whether it has happened before: the content is removed or never posted; a warning; chat or squad access is restricted; the account is suspended; the account is terminated. Serious cases skip straight to the end. We aim to be proportionate, and we will explain a decision if you ask.

Appeals

If you think a decision was wrong, email maxwell@gurrbrothers.com from the address on the account. A human will look at it.
