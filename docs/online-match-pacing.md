# Online match pacing

Where the dead air in an online match came from, and what now covers it. The
worst of it sat between the last pick of the draft and the game actually
starting — the point where a player has nothing left to do and is watching a
banner that does not change.

## The four sources

**1. Waiting out a poll interval after your own action.** The match watcher
(`watchMatch` in `js/online.js`) polls every 2s. That is right while waiting on
an opponent and wrong immediately after your own pick, skip or gameplan submit:
the server has already moved on and the client is only waiting to be told. Up
to two seconds of it at every round transition, and again at the end of the
draft.

`watchMatch` now returns `{ stop, poke }`. `poke()` reads immediately and stays
on a 350ms cadence for eight seconds, then falls back to the steady interval.
`pokeOnlineWatcher()` (`js/main.js`) is called after each submit. The extra
reads only happen in the seconds after this player has done something.

Poking rather than acting on what the submit RPC returns keeps **one** reader
of match state. A second path into those handlers is what they already guard
against being entered twice.

**2. The fallback poller starting cold.** `awaitSimulationStart` waited a flat
2s before its first read, and the likeliest single moment for a match to
already be ready is the instant it starts — the opponent submitted while this
player was still choosing. It now reads immediately and backs off 250ms →
2s, and gives up on time (`SIMULATION_WAIT_MS`) rather than on a try count.

**3. The Edge Function's cold start.** `simulate-match` boots a Deno isolate
and pulls the whole player table before it simulates anything, and all of it
used to happen *after* both gameplans were in, on a screen that says
"Simulating…".

Two changes, one on each side:

- `warmSimulator()` (`js/online.js`) is sent at the top of the strategy phase —
  the moment the draft ends — so the boot happens while the player is setting a
  rotation and picking a gameplan. Fire and forget: nothing waits on it, and a
  server that predates the warm path answers 400 and is warmed anyway. That
  matters, because the Edge Function deploys separately from the site.
- The function keeps its computed dataset summary for the life of the isolate
  (`datasetStatsFor` in `supabase/functions/simulate-match/index.ts`), TTL'd at
  10 minutes. The pool is regenerated by hand and published rarely, so minutes
  of staleness is the right trade for not recomputing a summary of the same
  public dataset on every request.

**4. Work done in series that could be done in parallel.** The final rosters
for the box score were fetched *after* the result arrived, though they have
nothing to do with the simulation. `runOnlineSimulationFlow` now starts that
load before the simulate call and awaits it after — and skips it entirely when
the strategy phase already built both rosters on this client, which it has
whenever the player drafted rather than reconnecting into the reveal.

## What was deliberately left alone

- **The steady 2s watcher interval.** Waiting on an opponent's pick is exactly
  what it is for, and polling faster would multiply reads across every match to
  save nothing.
- **The 25s result window** (`RESULT_WAIT_MS`). It exits the moment the row
  appears; only a genuinely stuck simulation pays it.
- **The intro's own nine seconds.** That is presentation, not waiting — see
  `docs/matchup-intro.md`.

## Known limitations

- The warm-up is best effort in the strictest sense: nothing guarantees the
  isolate that answers it is the one that answers the simulation. It is a
  probability play, and the dataset cache is what makes it pay off when it
  lands.
- A poke cannot help the player who submits *first*. They are genuinely waiting
  on their opponent, and no client-side change shortens that.
- None of this is measured by a test. The self-tests assert that the flow
  completes, not how long it took; the numbers above are structural (a removed
  round trip, a removed serial wait), not benchmarked.
